At a **summer party on company premises**, the **bar** often decides whether the evening feels easy and summery or turns into a long queue. Grill and salad complement the event – but most guests remember the **first spritz**, a **fresh mojito** and whether service at the counter was quick.

This **example** deliberately focuses on **cocktail operations**: setup, menu, timing and load at the bar. Starting point: a mid-sized company in the Oberberg region, **around 120 guests**, venue **courtyard and parking area** behind production. The figures are typical and can be adapted to other headcounts.

## Before the first shake: bar and setup

This is what the site looks like **before guests arrive**: two **mobile cocktail trailers**, the **bamboo bar** on the left, **seating areas** in the centre – still empty, but ready to go.

For 120 people over six hours, **two bartenders** at one central counter are usually enough. Before 4:00 p.m., the essentials are:

- **Crushed ice** and cooling in place – not a detail at summer parties, but a must  
- **Glassware, washing and waste** planned, not improvised  
- **Power** from the building agreed in advance; ice and ingredients brought by the mobile service  
- The **cocktail menu** displayed visibly – guests see what is available and order faster

**Demand depends on headcount and duration** – a manageable menu makes planning easier. **Water and soft drinks** should always be visible and easy to reach. In warm weather, **cooling and shade** for ice and fresh ingredients help drinks stay fresh through the afternoon.

**Why this works:** Serving from **4:30 p.m.** avoids the typical rush. **Open end** in the evening gives guests flexibility – **breakdown** follows once the last guests leave.

## When guests arrive: peak at the bar

<Image
  src={imageProvider.events.firmenfeierMediumCrowd.src}
  alt={getImageAlt(imageProvider.events.firmenfeierMediumCrowd, "en")}
/>

As the site **fills up**, demand at the bar rises too. That is when **clear processes** matter: a visible menu, a manageable selection and enough capacity at the counter. Non-alcoholic options belong on the menu by default – not as an extra, but as a fixed part of the offer.

If **food and drinks are sensibly distributed** across the site, the bar benefits as well. The goal is a relaxed flow where guests can order in peace – without long waits or lengthy discussions at the counter.
